# Impeccable — costCalcAI

## Design Context

### Users
Public, shared socially: r/LocalLLaMA / Hacker News / X readers, designers and
r/DataIsBeautiful-minded viewers who judge craft, and executives skimming for
the verdict. They arrive cold, often on a phone, primed to nitpick both the
methodology and the design. The job to be done: understand in under a minute
whether running local AI models is actually cheaper than cloud subscriptions —
and be able to cite the numbers afterward.

### Brand Personality
Authoritative, witty, evidence-first. Data-journalism register — The
Economist / FT Visual Journalism voice: opinionated conclusions earned by
visible methodology. Emotional goal: "this person did the homework, and the
punchline is delicious." Never breathless, never vendor-flavored.

### Aesthetic Direction
Editorial print poster (static, portrait, ~990px wide). Cream paper ground,
serif display headline with an orange square glyph, structured sections with
rules, restrained dataviz palette (blue #2a78d6, orange #eb6834, dark-orange
#b34418, green #0ca30c, yellow #eda100, red #d03b3b on ink #0b0b0b / sub
#52514e / mut #898781 / grid #e1e0d9), plus a hand-drawn red annotation layer
(cursive scrawl, scribbled ellipses) for the editorial voice.
Benchmark lineage: Economist Graphic Detail, FT Visual Journalism,
Information is Beautiful Award winners. Anti-references: dashboard
glassmorphism, dark-mode neon, hero-metric card grids, AI-slop gradients.

### Design Principles
1. **One chart, one claim** — every section leads with the sentence the chart
   proves; the reader should be able to quote it.
2. **Annotation is the voice, not the furniture** — at most two red hand-drawn
   notes per section; everything else is set type.
3. **Cite or cut** — every number traces to a dated source line; no orphan
   stats.
4. **Ink serves data** — recessive grids, direct labels over legends where
   possible, no decorative charts.
5. **The scrawl earns its ink** — hand-drawn marks only where the editorial
   punchline lives, never as texture.
