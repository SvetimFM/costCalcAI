demand. measured from the author’s own transcripts, jul 29 – aug 28 2026: 39.6M output · 513M new prefill (uncached input + cache writes) · 14.14B cache reads · 71,350 requests, deduplicated by message + request id.
the $14,914. the same token flow priced at anthropic list word-rates, every cache tier counted at its own price. the plan sold the month for $200 — 74.6× below its own meter.
desk feasibility. hours = (new prefill + 10% of cache reads) ÷ measured prefill speed + output ÷ decode speed. the 10% is kv-cache eviction a local server must recompute (scenarios run 5–25%); local caching is otherwise credited in full. a month holds 720 h.
desk cost. hardware amortized over 36 months net of resale (base residuals 40–70%, shortage-inflated) · capital at 5%/yr · power at measured idle/load watts × us-avg $0.183/kWh · tending 13.7 h/mo (setup share + upkeep + light interventions) at a chosen wage, $75/h base.
quality. artificial analysis intelligence index, same harness for all minds. 4-bit quantization costs ~4 points on every model measured. redo tax from a june 2026 paired study: 74 vs 114 tests passed (65%), 7× interventions. retry-adjusted hours = raw hours ÷ acceptance, with acceptance scaled from that 65% anchor by each mind’s quality index (q÷56 × 65%).
fair fight. the same token flow re-billed at posted open-model rates, checked aug 31–sep 1 2026: deepseek first-party off-peak with prefix-cache hits at 1–3% of list. routed hosts (openrouter): caching is pass-through and provider-dependent — reads at 0.25–0.5× input where supported, held by sticky routing, but measured tests show some hosts cache nothing; the $300–540 and $1,037 rows bracket those two worlds.

Measuring the “local models are free” claim against 14.69 billion tokens of my own usage
I parsed my own Claude Code transcripts for the 30 days ending August 28, 2026: 14.69 billion tokens across 71,350 requests, which would have cost $14,914 at API list rates but ran on a $200/mo subscription. This page asks whether buying hardware and running open models at home would have been cheaper. Short answer: no — once you count depreciation, power, tied-up capital, and the 13.7 hours a month of setup and upkeep, the hardware is a rounding error and your own time is 90% of the bill. The sections below walk through the workload, which machines could physically keep up, what each really costs per month, the quality gap, what renting the same open models costs instead, and the five-year picture.

96% of the workload is re-read memory — the race turns on the cache: first-party hosts re-serve it at 1–3% of list, routed hosts pass through 0.25–0.5× where supported and must be measured per model, and a host with no discount re-bills every re-read in full
Against these bills the desk wins some races: the $2,900 rig repays in 3–14 months against uncached rates, not 18. But wherever caching passes through, the rental undercuts even free-time ownership — owning only ties when the discount vanishes. Nothing touches the incumbent: no open mind, rented or owned, sells for $200 at frontier quality.

The conclusion: hardware was never the expensive part. The real cost of local AI for this workload is 13.7 hours a month of human attention — everything else is small change against a $200 subscription selling tokens at 74× below list price.
Running local still makes sense when a constraint forces it — data that can’t leave the building, workloads a subscription won’t cover, or hobby time you genuinely price at zero. Otherwise, the hours are the bill.
